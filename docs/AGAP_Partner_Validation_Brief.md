# AGAP partner-validation brief

Prepared for Team FinVerse, Team ID 53, University of Mindanao | 12 October 2026

Proposed review counterpart: DCCPEC Multipurpose Cooperative. AGAP is a concept under review. The cooperative's available member-support options are unknown. This brief is a discussion guide, not an agreement to adopt or pilot a system.

**Use this meeting to establish whether AGAP can improve an existing member-follow-up process.**

The proposed workflow is: verify payment information, identify a reason for follow-up, contact the member through existing procedures, record an authorized response, and check the outcome. Whether this can improve members' financial health remains a hypothesis.

Suggested meeting length: 35 minutes. Invite a staff member familiar with repayment records and, if available, someone familiar with follow-up procedures and approval responsibilities. Participants can decline questions or correct the team's understanding.

The proposal also lists Nabunturan Integrated Cooperative for confirmation. Its role remains unconfirmed. Keep any feedback from a second organization separate from DCCPEC's findings.

**Begin with this introduction (2 minutes).**

“We want to check whether AGAP addresses a real problem in your current process. We need only general descriptions of procedures and feedback on fictional examples. Please do not share member names, account details, repayment histories, or records. This discussion does not commit the cooperative to funding, adoption, or a live pilot.”

**Walk through the current process (8 minutes).**

1. How does staff notice that a payment record needs checking or a member may need follow-up?
2. Which record or system is authoritative, and how are delayed entries or corrections handled?
3. Who contacts the member, how is responsibility assigned, and how is the next follow-up recorded?
4. Where does the process become slow, repetitive, or dependent on one person's expertise?

Ask for descriptions of typical procedures rather than individual cases. Record the current steps and the bottleneck staff identifies. Ask which task AGAP could replace or simplify.

**Establish what staff can actually do (10 minutes).**

5. When a member reports difficulty paying, what actions, if any, are currently available?
6. Who may authorize each action, and what conditions or approvals apply?
7. If no assistance option applies, what is the permitted next step? How can staff explain the situation to the member?
8. How does staff determine whether follow-up is complete and whether the response was useful to the member?

Record options exactly as described. Do not treat a hypothetical payment adjustment, hardship service, or referral as an existing program. If staff cannot confirm a procedure, retain it as unknown and identify the appropriate role to clarify it.

**Test these fictional walkthroughs (10 minutes).**

These are invented design scenarios, not observations about the cooperative or its members. They test decision logic and usability, not predictive accuracy or financial-health impact.

| Fictional scenario | Proposed behavior to review | Ask staff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A payment was made, but its entry is delayed | Verify and correct the information before treating the case as payment difficulty | How would you establish that the record is current? |
| Payments are current, but the member requests help because they expect an income disruption | Record a support request and discuss only options confirmed as available | Can staff act before a missed payment? What authority is needed? |
| A member is overdue and declines contextual questions | Continue the ordinary follow-up process without inferring a cause from refusal | Can the member receive available assistance without explaining personal circumstances? |

For each case, ask staff to describe the next action, responsible role, approval requirement, and follow-up. If a proposed step conflicts with current policy or is impractical, record the correction. If feasible, have two staff members explain the workflow independently to reveal confusion or dependence on one expert.

**Close by checking your understanding (5 minutes).**

Read back the main bottleneck, available actions, responsible roles, and unresolved questions. Ask staff to correct the summary and identify which proposed step would be most useful. Record feedback as feedback; it does not constitute implementation approval.

**Use the findings to choose the product direction.**

| Partner finding | Team decision |
| --- | --- |
| Assistance exists, but cases reach staff late | Retain early warning and member support as the candidate scope; demonstrate one confirmed response pathway |
| Assistance exists, but assignments, approvals, or follow-ups stall | Focus the candidate scope on case ownership, coordination, and follow-up |
| No assistance pathway is confirmed, but record correction or follow-up has clear value | Evaluate a narrower operational tool; keep financial-health impact as an unproven hypothesis |
| Staff identifies no meaningful gap, or AGAP would add unacceptable work | Revisit the problem definition before expanding the product |

A discussion with staff can validate the problem and proposed process. It cannot establish actual borrower outcomes. A future live pilot would require a separate agreement and appropriate operational arrangements.
